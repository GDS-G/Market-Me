## Required outcomes

A current workspace owner/administrator should be able to review the effect of removing one other non-owner collaborator's workspace access, confirm the exact member and reason, and recover the original result after uncertainty. Protect self and all owner memberships; do not infer a safe last-owner transfer. Keep the account, other workspaces, personal sessions, content, source evidence, approvals, monetary history and provider credentials intact. Current access must be removed without rewriting historical attribution. New invitations/rejoining are separate explicit grants, never an automatic undo.

Explain precisely that revocation affects subsequent authorization, not already-admitted operations or externally delivered content. Do not silently cancel campaigns, delete provider output, change ownership or reassign inbox responsibilities to an arbitrary person. Any required reassignment or execution-policy choice must be visible and explicit in the application.

## Design work required before mutation

Inventory every workspace_membership foreign key, actor/target authority query, cached capability, worker creator dependency, invitation upsert, current-member role mutation and exact recovery path. Several immutable/history tables currently RESTRICT membership deletion, while some inbox/read-state references CASCADE. A raw DELETE would either fail or destroy records. Do not drop those constraints, cascade history or weaken workspace lineage merely to make a Remove button work.

Separate historical attribution from current authorization using an explicit reviewed schema design. Determine which rows are historical, which are current assignments and which are disposable per-member state. Preserve original before/after evidence and require current permissions for new actions. Prove existing grants and history survive upgrade before accepting a migration. Test retained policy, budget, quote, exception, approval, inbox and AI records rather than only an empty new member.

Introduce an incarnation-aware membership identity/revision boundary for remove/rejoin. An old role-change or removal form must not mutate a newly invited membership merely because its counter reset to1. Preserve legacy receipts as historical facts without inventing an original incarnation for records that cannot prove one. Version request/response compatibility explicitly; never silently reinterpret old pending intent. Existing role-change provenance and actor-private recovery require particular review.

## Authority, atomicity and UI

Use current independent owner/admin authority, deterministic actor/target row lock order, expected target incarnation/revision, a bounded reason and exact UUID intent. Account or organization ownership alone must not become workspace authority. Serialize competing role/removal/rejoin requests and verify both ordering outcomes. Commit access removal, retained original receipt and minimized workspace audit atomically; rollback any failure without losing access/history. Replay must require current appropriate actor authority and never remove a later incarnation.

Show an impact preview with truthful known dependencies and unresolved choices, then a separate explicit confirmation. Keep preview nonmutating and revalidate every assumption at commit. Use private bounded transport, strict scope/projection and memory or existing privacy-reviewed recovery that never automatically resends after uncertainty. Distinguish current access state from historical removal outcome. Do not expose private cross-workspace records or user credentials in previews.

## Acceptance

Cover all role matrices, foreign/self/owner targets, current authority changes, legacy/member incarnation upgrades, remove/rejoin stale forms, actor/target races, invitation conflicts, exact retries, history preservation, audit rollback and representative downstream authorization. Use synthetic accounts and isolated databases only. Real people must not lose access during development. Document every schema/DTO/constant/collection/lifetime, migration compatibility decision and operational boundary in repository references and the existing Google development sections. Whole-product and production offboarding acceptance remain distinct from a passing isolated test.

## Initial dependency review (not an implemented design)

The live 124-migration CI schema has 63 membership foreign keys across 38 tables and no dependent membership views: 56 RESTRICT, four NO ACTION and three CASCADE. Existing membership foreign keys span AI analysis/quotes/reservations/exceptions/alerts, provider/adapter/policy/execution/incident records, invitation attribution and conversation policies/resources/drafts/suggestions/legal holds/erasure. Read-state, drafting-presence and review-mention references cascade on physical membership deletion; the other provenance references restrict or defer deletion. Historical FKs must remain intact, not be retargeted to an active-only grant relation.

The database source contains 62 membership references outside tests: four insertion sites, one role-update site and the remaining authority, active-team/assignee, routing or scoped-recovery reads (the composer also uses membership in a DELETE ... USING authorization predicate). HTTP authorization and workspace selection route through the core getWorkspaceAccess/listWorkspaceAccess projection. Current actor/target validation also occurs in AI, conversation, source preparation, presets, package review, workflow preparation/finalization and catalog repositories. SQL receipt/binding guards from migrations 0116–0121 independently check membership; updating TypeScript alone is insufficient.

A candidate design retains workspace_membership as historical identity and introduces an explicitly named active_workspace_membership view for current authority. It would add a revoked timestamp and grant-incarnation UUID, retain all preexisting columns/FKs, and make every authority read explicitly use active grants. Historical records continue to reference the permanent workspace/user identity. This avoids renaming an existing table into a silently different relation, rewriting old migrations or cascading provenance. The view must not be described as protection from arbitrary trusted SQL; database privileges and normal server authorization remain separate boundaries. PostgreSQL's [view semantics](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-createview.html) and [row-locking rules](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-select.html) must be verified in live contention tests, not assumed from compilation.

Retained membership revisions should advance monotonically for revoke/regrant as well as actual role changes; they must never restart at one. A new grant incarnation distinguishes a fresh invitation from the old access period. Existing role requests can retain their legacy exact-byte receipt format only if the monotonic counter conclusively rejects a stale uncommitted edit after rejoin; legacy receipts must not gain invented incarnation facts. New removal requests should bind both current incarnation and expected revision. Direct deletion/recreation needs an integrity guard or an equally strong persistent revision boundary, including workspace/account cascade compatibility.

OIDC acceptance currently upserts organization membership and inserts workspace membership ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING. It must distinguish an already-active member from an explicitly re-invited revoked member. Creation, revocation and acceptance of invitations must be coordinated with removal: an old pending invitation cannot silently undo offboarding. Review lock ordering before implementation, particularly invitation-row locks, actor/target membership locks and OIDC account locks. Do not introduce a workspace mutex halfway through a flow that already holds a conflicting row lock.

Impact preview must distinguish historical creators from current operational assignments. Inspect conversation_thread.assigned_owner_id, enabled routing target_owner_id, pending requested reviewers/assigned campaign approvers, destination ownership, queued source commands and AI/workflow requester dependencies. Removing access must not arbitrarily reassign, cancel, erase or transfer these. Either show the precise retained/blocked behavior or require explicit resolution through existing product controls; revalidate at commit and cover concurrent assignment/invitation creation. The final blocking-versus-warning policy is not yet implemented or accepted.
